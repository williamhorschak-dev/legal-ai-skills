# Worked example: the model memo

An objective research memorandum written by a legal assistant to supervising
attorneys. It is the model for the default memo form described in `memoranda.md`,
the way the Doe sever motion in `worked-example.md` is the model for the default
filing form. Read that file first if you have not; this one assumes its vocabulary.

This is a well-built memo, and it is well built in ways that are teachable.
The annotations are what matters. Only short fragments are quoted, because the
underlying memo is a privileged document; where a move is described rather
than quoted, the description is the anchor.

That is the source example's claimed status, not a determination of privilege for
a new memo. Use only authorized source material. Do not transfer the example's
legend, facts, signer identity, supervision, or verification claims unless they
are true for the current assignment; follow `memoranda.md` §§ 3 and 11.

---

## The skeleton

```
Legend, letterspaced small caps: Privileged and Confidential · Attorney Work Product
MEMORANDUM (letterspaced, centered, double rule beneath)
To: / From: / Date: / Re:      <- Re runs two full lines
Conventions note block          <- smaller type, before the first heading
I.   Questions Presented        <- numbered; each carries its statutory or doctrinal hook
II.  Brief Answers              <- numbered to match; each opens with a bold calibrated verdict
III. Facts                      <- provenance stated in the opening; outside reporting quarantined
IV.  Discussion                 <- A through G; full-sentence headings in bold italic
V.   Conclusion and Recommendations  <- assessment paragraph, then numbered action steps
Closing note                    <- first person, candid
Respectfully submitted, / signature rule / name in bold / Legal Assistant
Running header on continuation pages; Page X of Y footer
```

Note what is not there: no Legal Standard section, no table of contents, no
executive summary duplicating the Brief Answers, no footnotes. Seven
discussion subsections and every one earns its letter.

---

## 1. The legend comes before everything, including the title

> Privileged and Confidential · Attorney Work Product

**Why it works.** The first line of the document, before MEMORANDUM, before
the To line, asserts the document's status, which is where a court assessing
reasonable precautions under Wis. Stat. § 905.03(5) would want to see it, and
where a hurried colleague deciding whether to forward it cannot miss it. The
letterspaced small caps read as a standing formal notice rather than a
decoration. The label is also accurate for its situation: this writer is a
nonlawyer employee working under counsel, so "Attorney Work Product" is the right
phrase, where a pro se document would drop the word "attorney."

**The move to steal:** status first, content second. The legend is the one
line that must survive any excerpting, so it goes where excerpting starts.

## 2. The Re line does the work of an abstract

The Re line runs two full lines: the matter, the parties, and what the memo
decides.

**Why it works.** Eighteen months from now this memo will be found by
searching a document system, and the Re line is what the search returns. A
topic ("Re: notice question") makes the memo findable only by the person who
wrote it. Two lines naming the matter, the parties, and the deciding question
make it findable by anyone, and they scope the memo honestly: a reader can
tell from the Re line alone whether this memo answers their question or
merely touches it.

**The move to steal:** write the Re line as if it were the only part anyone
will ever read in the file index, because it is.

## 3. The conventions block, which is the best thing in the memo

Before the first heading, in smaller type, the memo declares its own rules:
that quotation marks enclose only verbatim judicial, AG, statutory, or
constitutional language transcribed from the source documents; that initial
capitalization and terminal punctuation conform to Bluebook R. 5.2 and all
other alterations are bracketed or elided; the statutory edition and the Act
through which it is updated, with the publication date; that decisions
predating a renumbering cite the old subsection, with the current one given;
a pointer to a closing note on quotation verification and pinpoints; and a
statement that the memo was adversarially cite-checked against the filed
source PDFs before circulation, with the items still needing reporter or
Lexis confirmation listed at the end.

**Why it works.** Every one of these declarations converts a class of silent
assumptions into checkable warranties. The quotation-marks rule means the
reader can trust every quoted phrase or catch the writer lying; there is no
middle state. The edition line dates the law, so the memo carries its own
expiry information. The renumbering note defuses the specific Wisconsin trap
where an accurately quoted old case cites a subsection that has since moved.
And the cite-check sentence does the most work of all, because it does not
claim perfection: it claims a process, and it lists what the process has not
yet reached. A nonlawyer cannot promise the law is right. A nonlawyer can
promise exactly what was checked, against what, and what remains. That is the
honest version of authority, and it is more useful to a supervising attorney
than confidence would be.

This block is why the memo can be trusted at speed, which is the whole economy
of a memo.

**The move to steal:** declare your verification method up front and your
unverified residue at the end, by name. The template is in `memoranda.md` § 3.

## 4. Questions Presented carry their hooks

Each numbered question is a full question with its statutory or doctrinal hook
inside it, on the under/does/when pattern.

**Why it works.** A question that names its governing rule is answerable and
checkable; a topic is neither. Because each question carries its own hook, the
Brief Answers can open with a verdict instead of first reconstructing what was
asked, and the Discussion headings can inherit the same order. The three
sections lock together, and the reader can navigate by number from any one of
them to the other two.

**The move to steal:** the question, the answer, and the discussion heading
are one thought at three depths. Draft them together and keep them numbered
in lockstep.

## 5. The verdicts are calibrated, and none of them is a bare yes

The six Brief Answers open, in bold: "Probably yes," "Not on this record,"
"Neither," "Yes as to the County, on the record as it stands," "No on current
facts," "Not realistically."

**Why it works.** Six questions, six different verdicts, and not one is an
unqualified yes or no. Each verdict is fitted to its question rather than
drawn from a two-item menu. "Not on this record" and "No on current facts"
scope the prediction to the fact base, which tells the attorney the answer is
contingent and where the contingency lives. "Yes as to the County, on the
record as it stands" splits the answer by party, which a bare yes would have
falsified. "Neither" refuses a false binary the question could have invited.
"Not realistically" is a candid practical judgment distinct from a legal
impossibility, and the distinction is information. A supervising attorney
reading only this section learns not just the answers but the shape of the
uncertainty behind each one.

**The move to steal:** let the verdict carry its own condition. "Not on this
record" is four words that do the work of a paragraph of hedging, without
hedging.

**One improvement on the model:** the memo uses this vocabulary without
defining it, so the reader calibrates by feel. `memoranda.md` § 5 defines the
ladder; use it, and the verdicts become measurements instead of impressions.

## 6. The Facts open by pricing themselves

The Facts section opens by naming the source of the facts and stating:

> They are reported, not established.

Outside public reporting is then quarantined in its own paragraph, flagged as
needing verification before use.

**Why it works.** Five words, first paragraph, and every fact that follows is
correctly discounted before it is read. The memo never has to hedge
individual sentences with "allegedly" thickets, because the provenance was
set wholesale at the top and departures from it are marked. The quarantine
paragraph is the same discipline applied at the next tier down: press
accounts are useful for orientation and dangerous as evidence, so they are
present, visible, and fenced. This is SKILL.md's never-invent-a-record-fact
rule operating in reverse: not only does the writer add no facts, he
subtracts unearned confidence from the ones he has.

**The move to steal:** state the provenance of the whole fact section in its
first sentence, then fence anything below that grade in its own flagged
paragraph. A fact section that does this can be acted on; one that does not
must first be interrogated.

## 7. The Discussion headings predict

Sections A through G, each heading a full-sentence proposition in bold
italic.

**Why it works.** Seven letters is a long walk, and the full-sentence
headings are what make it navigable: read in sequence, they are the analysis
in miniature, a built-in table of contents that carries conclusions instead
of topics. Bold italic distinguishes them from the bold verdicts in the Brief
Answers, so the eye can tell the two layers apart when flipping. The genre
shift from the Doe motion's point headings is done correctly: these
headings predict ("the court would likely..."), they do not urge ("the court
should...").

**The move to steal:** test the Discussion by reading only its headings. If
the headings alone do not deliver the analysis and its direction, they are
labels, and labels are the first thing `memoranda.md` § 15 lists under
Discussion failures.

## 8. The Conclusion converts analysis into a work plan

An assessment paragraph first, then numbered next steps, each opening with a
bolded action label carrying an urgency tag: "(immediate)," "(now)."

**Why it works.** The assessment paragraph is the memo's judgment in prose,
kept separate from the steps so the reader can see where analysis ends and
recommendation begins. That organization helps attorney review; it does not
establish a UPL exception. The numbered steps are addressed to the attorney, and
each is concrete enough to assign. The urgency tags rank the list without a
paragraph of explanation: two words in parentheses tell the reader what
cannot wait for the next status meeting. A memo that ends this way gets acted
on the day it is read.

**The move to steal:** end with verbs the attorney can delegate, ranked by
when. An unranked list of recommendations is a second reading assignment; a
ranked one is a plan.

## 9. The closing note says what was not found

A short first-person note, before the signature, saying candidly what the
writer hoped to find and did not.

**Why it works.** This is the paragraph that proves the memo predicts rather
than argues. A writer who wanted a particular answer, looked for it, failed
to find it, and says so has demonstrated the neutrality audit ("would you
write the same analysis for the other side?") on the page. It also transmits
real information: the absence the writer names is an absence the attorney no
longer needs to spend time searching for, and it invites the attorney to
redirect the search if the writer looked in the wrong place. First person is
correct here and only here; the analysis is impersonal, the report of the
research effort is not.

**The move to steal:** one honest paragraph about the hole in your research
buys more credibility than any amount of confidence in the rest.

## 10. The signature tells the truth about the writer

> Respectfully submitted,

then the signature rule, the name in bold, and beneath it:

> Legal Assistant

**Why it works.** The From line and signature block accurately identify the
writer's nonlawyer role. Routing the memo to the supervising attorney supports
review, but neither the title nor the addressee establishes compliance by itself.
Reliance on SCR 23.02(2)(m) requires actual supervision consistent with SCR 20:5.3;
see [the UPL guidance](memoranda.md#12-the-upl-boundary). Privilege and work-product
protection also depend on the facts, not the label. A running header and
`Page X of Y` footer help a reviewer recognize missing pages.

**The move to steal:** the honest title, everywhere the document identifies
its author, together with an accurate review status and actual supervision.

## Things this memo does not do

The absences are deliberate, and they track `memoranda.md` § 15.

- No advice to the client, and no sentence addressed to one. Every
  recommendation runs to the attorneys.
- No bare "Yes" or "No" anywhere in the Brief Answers. Every verdict carries
  its calibration or its condition.
- No fact stated above its provenance. Reported facts are called reported;
  public reporting is fenced.
- No claimed certainty about verification. The conventions block promises a
  process and lists its residue; it does not promise the law is right.
- No topic headings. All seven Discussion headings are propositions.
- No executive summary, no boilerplate blanket disclaimer footer, no
  restatement of the Brief Answers dressed as a Conclusion. The Conclusion
  adds judgment and next steps or it would not exist.
- No footnotes doing substantive work.
- No indignation, no adjectives about any party, no "clearly."

## What would make it better

Three things, all improvements on an already good form.

1. **Define the calibration vocabulary.** The verdicts are excellent and
   undefined. A one-line key in the conventions block, or a standing default
   ladder the office adopts once (see `memoranda.md` § 5), turns "Probably
   yes" from an impression into a term with agreed content, and lets two
   memos by two writers be compared.
2. **Promote sensitivity analysis to its own numbered section.** The memo's
   record-scoped verdicts ("Not on this record," "No on current facts") imply
   the writer knows exactly which facts are load-bearing. A section between
   Discussion and Conclusion stating them: each load-bearing fact, its
   source, what flips if it is wrong; the open facts and what would establish
   them; the missing documents and who has them; any legal instability. The
   material half exists in the verdicts already; giving it a section makes it
   assignable. See `memoranda.md` § 9.
3. **State the work product basis and the review status.** The legend asserts
   the conclusion ("Attorney Work Product") but not its condition. Adding
   "prepared in anticipation of litigation" and, if true, "at the direction
   of counsel," plus the one-sentence nonlawyer review legend from
   `memoranda.md` § 12, makes the legend recite the facts that actually
   support the protection under Wis. Stat. § 804.01(2)(c)1, and makes
   explicit that the memo becomes advice only after attorney review.

## The one habit worth naming

The memo's signature quality is that it prices everything: facts by
provenance, answers by confidence, citations by verification status, next
steps by urgency. Nothing is presented at a certainty it has not earned. That
is the entire objective-memo genre in one habit, and it is the habit the
conventions block, the quarantine paragraph, and the calibrated verdicts all
serve. Steal the habit before the features.
