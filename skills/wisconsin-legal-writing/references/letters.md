# Letters to the court

The default letter carries **no caption**, unless the court requires one. Its form is a business
letter with a case reference line. They are docketed and served like any other
paper, assume everything in a letter to the court is on the public record and
goes to every other party.

## Form

```
[MONTH DAY, YEAR]

The Honorable [JUDGE NAME]
Circuit Court Judge
[COUNTY] County Circuit Court
[(Acting on assignment for [COUNTY] County Circuit Court)]

     Re:  [COUNTY] County Case No. 25CV123

Dear Judge [NAME]:

[Opening sentence states the purpose of the letter in one line. "I respectfully
advise the Court that…" / "I write to clarify…" / "I write in response to the
Court's letter of ___."]

[Body. Two to five short paragraphs. Facts and procedural posture only.]

[Closing sentence stating what, if anything, the writer is asking for, or
expressly stating that no relief is sought.]

Respectfully,


[SIGNATURE APPROPRIATE TO FILING METHOD]
[NAME]
[ROLE AND REQUIRED CONTACT INFORMATION]
```

Notes on the form as used in the corpus:

- The case reference line is indented and uses the **short case number**
  (`25CV123`), not the long form.
- Where a judge is sitting on assignment, say so in the address block, it tells
  the clerk which file the letter belongs in.
- `Respectfully,` not `Sincerely,` or `Very truly yours,`.
- Use `Electronically signed by [NAME]` or an imaged handwritten signature for
  eFiling; paper parties use a handwritten signature. Include the actual role and
  required contact information. See `signature-blocks.md` and §§ 801.18(12), 802.05(1).
- Some letters carry the case number in the top right of page 1 as a running
  identifier. That is a convenience, not a requirement.

## What a letter may do

- Advise the court of a scheduling conflict or a correction.
- Transmit a document or respond to the court's own letter.
- Record an objection for the record where no motion is pending
  (`Letter to the Court RE: Recorded Objections`).
- Clarify which of two related matters an earlier communication concerned.
- Request a ruling on a fully briefed motion.

## Email is not a letter, and in La Crosse it never goes to the judge

**La Crosse County Local Rule 436:** "In order to preserve impartiality and fairness
in all judicial proceedings, all email correspondence regarding cases before the
court shall be addressed and sent to the appropriate judge's judicial assistant, and
not to the judge directly."

So: correspondence that belongs in the record gets eFiled as a letter. Scheduling
and administrative email goes to the judicial assistant. Nothing goes to the judge's
own address. Copy every other party on both.

## What a letter may not do

- Substitute for a motion. If it asks the court to order something, it is a motion
  in disguise, and the court can and often should disregard it. Wis. Stat.
  § 802.01(2)(a) requires an application for an order to be **by motion**, in
  writing, stating the grounds with particularity and the relief sought.
- Argue the merits of a pending motion. That is what the brief is for.
- Communicate ex parte. Copy every party, every time.

## Style

Shorter than a brief and less formal, but not casual. No headings. No WHEREFORE.
Numbered paragraphs only where the letter transmits a list. Keep it to one page
where possible; a letter that runs three pages should probably have been a motion.
