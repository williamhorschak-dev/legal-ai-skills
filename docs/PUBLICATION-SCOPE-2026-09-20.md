# Publication scope and notice review — September 20, 2026

The maintainer confirmed that he is not a licensed attorney and publishes tools
only. The project now states that scope prominently, excludes individual case
advice from support, and warns against posting confidential material. These are
publication and support boundaries, not a legal opinion that a particular service
or deployment complies with unauthorized-practice rules.

## Primary texts consulted

- [Wisconsin SCR chapter 23](https://www.wicourts.gov/sc/scrule/DisplayDocument.pdf?content=pdf&seqNo=692090):
  SCR 23.01 defines the practice of law; SCR 23.02 provides specific exceptions and
  title restrictions. Paragraph (2)(w) covers general legal information while
  excluding advice directed to a specific person in a specific matter. Paragraph
  (2)(m) conditions nonlawyer assistance on lawyer supervision and SCR 20:5.3.
- [SCR 20:5.3](https://www.wicourts.gov/sc/scrule/DisplayDocument.pdf?content=pdf&seqNo=1121033),
  printed pages 179–180: supervision duties and the conditions for a lawyer's
  responsibility for nonlawyer conduct. A review label does not supply supervision.
- [Wis. Stat. § 757.30](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/757.pdf):
  unauthorized practice and holding out. The official chapter PDF states currency
  through 2025 Wis. Act 247 and orders effective September 4, 2026. Retrieval on
  September 20 does not establish developments after that stated cutoff.

The chapter 23 PDF was reached through the court's
[current rules index](https://www.wicourts.gov/supreme/sc_rules.jsp). The listed
chapter release date is July 19, 2012; it is the chapter linked by that index.
This was a focused primary-text check. No case-treatment audit, exhaustive search
of later orders, or individualized professional legal opinion was performed.

## Implemented safeguards and limits

The README, standalone skill notices, and issue form identify the maintainer as
a nonlawyer. Public support accepts technical defects, general corrections, and
feature suggestions using synthetic examples. Contribution instructions exclude
individualized case advice. Validation checks that all three skill notices match
the canonical root notice; packages and prompt exports carry that notice.

Interpretation and memorandum guidance no longer implies that labels, routing,
or leaving a decision to the requester alone establish a UPL exception. An
unsupported claim about weak enforcement and an unverified secondary quotation
were removed. The MIT license and document layouts are unchanged.

The conclusion that a disclaimer cannot establish a blanket AI/software exemption
is a cautious inference from the rules' conduct-based definitions and limited
exceptions, not an AI-specific holding. The notices do not create a technical
barrier to someone posting improper content, and they cannot guarantee immunity.
Actual support conduct must match them. Before offering individualized assistance,
a hosted advice service, or a materially different business model, obtain advice
from a Wisconsin attorney familiar with UPL and professional-responsibility law.
